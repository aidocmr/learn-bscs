### How Did William James Define the Material Self?

The material self consists of everything a person considers theirs: body, clothes, immediate family, home, and possessions.

### What is the Hierarchy of the Material Self?

1. **Body**: Innermost core of the material self.

2. **Clothes**: Expresses identity and status ("Philosophy of Dress").

3. **Immediate Family**: Relatives who reflect our personal worth and identity.

4. **Home**: Primary haven and nest of selfhood.

### Utility vs. Significance in Goods Acquisition

- **Utility**: The practical, functional purpose an object serves.

- **Significance**: The symbolic meaning, social status, or ritual value assigned to an object.

### What is Consumerism and Conspicuous Consumption?

- **Consumerism**: Social order encouraging continuous acquisition of goods.

- **Conspicuous Consumption**: Purchasing luxury items specifically to display wealth or status.

## Summary

The material self reflects how physical items, clothing, and environment extend human identity. As conceptualized by William James, individuals extend their identity into their possessions, homes, and relationships. In modern consumer culture, purchases often shift from practical need (utility) to identity signaling and status (significance).

## Main Notes

### Structure of the Material Self (William James)

- **Subclasses**:
- _Bodily Self_: Intimate physical parts, cognitive abilities, moral beliefs.

- _Extended Self_: Significant people (family), physical property, workspace, products of labor.

### Economic Identity & Consumer Behavior

- **"I Shop Therefore I Am"**: Conceptual phrase by Barbara Kruger highlighting how modern identity is often defined by ownership rather than thought.

- **Belk's Extended Self (1988)**: _"We are what we have and what we possess."_

- **Needs vs. Wants**:
- _Needs_: Essential items for survival.

- _Wants_: Desires influenced by social trends, lifestyle choices, and status.
