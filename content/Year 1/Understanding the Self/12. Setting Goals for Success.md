### What is Bandura's Theory of Self-Efficacy?

Self-efficacy is the belief in one's capability to succeed in specific tasks. Strengthened through:

1. _Mastery Experiences_ (past successes).

2. _Vicarious Experiences_ (role models).

3. _Verbal Persuasion_ (encouragement).

4. _Physiological/Emotional States_ (stress management).

### Fixed Mindset vs. Growth Mindset (Carol Dweck)

- **Fixed Mindset**: Believes intelligence and talent are unchangeable traits.

- **Growth Mindset**: Believes abilities can be developed through dedication, practice, and hard work.

### What is Rotter's Locus of Control?

- **Internal Locus**: Belief that personal outcomes are controlled by one's own efforts.

- **External Locus**: Belief that outcomes are determined by fate, luck, or external forces.

### What are Edwin Locke's 5 Principles of Goal Setting?

1. **Clarity**: Clear and measurable goals.

2. **Challenge**: Moderately difficult tasks that boost motivation.

3. **Commitment**: Personal dedication to the objective.

4. **Feedback**: Ongoing tracking and performance adjustments.

5. **Task Complexity**: Managing steps to avoid becoming overwhelmed.

## Summary

Goal achievement is guided by psychological mindset and structured planning. Cultivating a growth mindset and strong self-efficacy empowers individuals to view challenges as learning opportunities. Applying Locke's goal-setting principles within a SMART framework ensures clear direction, high commitment, and sustained motivation.

## Main Notes

### Key Goal-Setting Framework (SMART)

- **S**pecific: Well-defined objectives.

- **M**easurable: Quantifiable tracking criteria.

- **A**chievable: Challenging yet attainable targets.

- **R**elative/Relevant: Aligned with long-term aspirations.

- **T**ime-bound: Clear deadlines.

### Psychological Theories of Achievement

- **Albert Bandura**: Efficacy expectations determine how long people persist when facing obstacles.

- **Carol Dweck**: Embracing a Growth Mindset transforms failure into valuable feedback.

- **Julian Rotter**: Cultivating an Internal Locus of Control increases personal responsibility and academic resilience.
